## Know Your System's Age

Homes built in different phases of Riverchase got different systems. A 1990s install and a 2010s install differ in tank materials, field sizing standards, and baffle designs. The older the system, the more important regular inspection becomes, because components like steel lids and early-generation filters wear out on a schedule.

Check your permit record with the Jefferson County Department of Health. The file shows install date, tank size, and field layout. If you bought the home resale and never saw the record, get it now. It is the single most useful document in septic ownership, and it costs nothing to request. While you are at it, note the field location on a simple sketch and keep it with your home records. Knowing where not to park, plant trees, or build additions prevents most accidental field damage.

## Riverchase Septic Service: Avoiding the Big Bill

Drain field replacement is the expense everyone fears, and most of it is preventable. Fields die from solids escaping the tank, which happens when pumping is skipped, or from hydraulic overload, which happens when water use exceeds design. Both are maintenance problems, not mysteries.

A reliable riverchase septic service will tell you honestly which risk applies to your system. If the tank is structurally sound and the field is just stressed, pumping, filter service, and water-use adjustments can add years. A good riverchase septic service fixes what is fixable and only recommends replacement when the field has truly failed.
