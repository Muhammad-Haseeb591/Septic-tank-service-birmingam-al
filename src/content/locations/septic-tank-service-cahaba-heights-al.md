## Older Homes, Older Systems

A tank installed in the 1960s or 1970s has lived a full life, and plenty are still in the ground around Cahaba Heights. Concrete tanks from that era can deteriorate from the inside. Steel tanks rust through. Drain fields laid decades ago were not sized for modern water use, and the red clay common in Jefferson County makes undersized fields fail sooner.

Start with the permit record. The Jefferson County Department of Health handles septic permits and site evaluations, and the file tells you tank size, field layout, and install date. If no record exists, assume the system predates modern permitting and get an inspection before spending money on anything else.

## Renovations Change the Load

Cahaba Heights has seen steady remodeling as buyers update older homes. Adding bathrooms, a laundry room, or a kitchen expansion increases what the system must handle. A tank and field sized for a three-bedroom house in 1972 may not keep up with a modern household's water use.

A quick evaluation before a remodel beats a failed system after one. If you are planning work, have the system inspected first and ask specifically whether the field can handle the added load. This one step saves more money than any other in septic ownership.

## Cahaba Heights Septic FAQs

**My home was remodeled. Does that affect the septic?**
Yes. Added bathrooms and fixtures increase wastewater volume. Have the system evaluated to confirm the field can handle the new load.
